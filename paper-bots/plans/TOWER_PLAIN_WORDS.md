# Every tower in plain words

Short explanations for each tower's card (checklist steps 505, 564, 588, 613, 636, 649, 661, 685, 697, 709, 721, 733). Pretend money only.

## Why a high win rate can still lose money (Commodity 15m, step 505)
When a tower buys at 85 cents, a win pays 15 cents and a loss costs 85 cents. One loss wipes out about five and a half wins. On Nov 12 Commodity 15m won 177 of 232 bets and still lost $52. Win rate alone says nothing; the card should always show average win vs average loss. The Result splits building flags every group like this as "wins often, loses money".

## BTC carry (step 564)
It owns Bitcoin and, at the same time, bets the same amount against Bitcoin on the perpetual market. The price moves cancel out. What is left is the funding fee that perpetual traders pay each other every 8 hours. When funding is positive, the short side gets paid. It costs 0.30% to open, so it only works if funding stays positive for a while. A red month is noted, not acted on.

## Fed watch (step 588)
Kalshi and Polymarket both sell bets on what the Fed will do at its next meeting. When the same outcome is cheaper on Kalshi than Polymarket says it should be, by at least 5 cents after the fee, it buys on Kalshi. Both must settle on the same Fed decision.

## Point spread and Game total (step 613)
These read the Kalshi books for "will this team win by more than X" and "will both teams score more than Y in total". They buy nothing yet, because there is no fair-price model. They only log the books.

## Gas weekly (step 636)
Kalshi asks whether the AAA national average gas price will be above a line on a set day. The tower takes today's AAA price, assumes the recent trend slowly fades, and prices each line. It buys when Kalshi is at least 5 cents cheap after the fee.

## Crypto ladder (step 649)
Kalshi sells "Bitcoin above $X at 5 PM ET" for many prices X. The tower uses the price and the options market's view of how much Bitcoin may move to price every line, and buys the ones at least 5 cents cheap after the fee.

## Nasdaq (step 661)
Same idea for the Nasdaq-100 close at 4 PM ET, using the live index and its fear gauge (VXN). The floor is only 2 cents, so fees and slippage matter a lot.

## Favorite harvest (step 685)
Heavy favorites (88 to 94 cents) close to the end often win a bit more often than their price says. The tower would buy them only where the calibration lab has seen at least 50 settled cases and the cautious low end still beats the price. It is off until then. A favorite loss costs about ten wins.

## 10-year Treasury (step 697)
Kalshi asks where the official 10-year Treasury yield will land today. The tower reads the live market yield, adds its usual daily move and the measured gap to the official number, and buys only on settlement day.

## CPI and GDP nowcast (step 709)
The Cleveland Fed and the Atlanta Fed publish daily guesses of the next inflation and growth numbers. The towers turn those guesses, plus how wrong they usually are, into prices for each Kalshi line. They stop buying 2 hours before the release.

## Soccer & tennis lean (step 721)
Polymarket often has more money on soccer and tennis winners than Kalshi. When Kalshi is at least 6 cents cheaper than Polymarket's price, before the match starts, it buys on Kalshi. A gap over 15 cents is treated as "something is off" (lineup or injury news) and only logged.

## QQQ momentum (step 733)
Pretend QQQ shares. If QQQ breaks out of its normal daily range at a half-hour check, it goes with the move, trails a stop and is always flat by the close. It pays a commission and 1 cent of slippage per share.

## The new support buildings
- Result splits: cuts each tower's results by market, exit, hour and side. Shows which leagues, coins or scan times earn their keep.
- Risk room: shows when several towers are making the same bet.
- Freshness inspector: shows each building's last and next run and flags late ones.
